## 1. Which Virco desk is it?

The label says Virco. Virco sells three tablet-arm chair desks with a chrome side arm and a wire book rack, and they differ mostly in the seat:

| model | seat and back | what to look for |
|---|---|---|
| **3700BRM** (3000 Series), most likely | separate hard-plastic seat and back, no vent holes | a gap between seat and back; back held on chrome tubes |
| 9700BR (9000 Series) | one-piece shell with three vertical slots in the back | slots, no gap |
| 2700BR (2000 Series) | one-piece shell with one triangular hole low in the back | single keyhole-shaped vent |

The photos show a separate navy back on chrome tubes and a single arm on the right, which matches the **3700BRM**. The 2400/3400/9400 "combo desks" were ruled out because their tops sit on two front legs rather than one side arm.

To check in person, look under the seat for the moulded model number and look at where the seat meets the back.

## 2. Body sizes

There is no current, public, percentile table of sitting measurements for US teenagers. The classic dataset (Snyder et al., 1977) is out of date, and the replacement, YouthShape.US from the University of Michigan (data collection 2024-2026), was not published yet. So I used the 2012 US Army survey (ANSUR II, Gordon et al. 2014) as the stand-in, taking its 5th percentile woman as a smaller high-schooler and its 95th percentile man as a larger one. Source table: NC State Ergonomics Center summary, https://ergocenter.ncsu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/358/2025/11/Anthropometry-Summary-Table-2020.pdf

| cm (no shoes) | F 5th | F 50th | M 50th | M 95th |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Popliteal height (floor to back of knee) | 35.0 | 38.7 | 43.0 | 47.1 |
| Buttock to back of knee | 44.1 | 48.3 | 50.2 | 54.8 |
| Buttock to front of knee | 54.1 | 58.9 | 61.7 | 66.9 |
| Hip breadth, sitting | 31.1 | 35.3 | 34.4 | 38.7 |
| Thigh thickness (seat to top of thigh) | 14.5 | 16.7 | 18.0 | 20.7 |
| Elbow height above the seat | 18.9 | 23.3 | 24.6 | 29.1 |
| Shoulder height above the seat | 51.5 | 56.4 | 60.3 | 65.3 |
| Abdominal depth, sitting | 18.4 | 22.7 | 25.1 | 32.0 |

Army personnel are fitter than the average student, so the larger-body case uses clinical data for high-BMI adults (PMC6820124): sitting hip breadth averages 54.5 cm for women, with a 95th percentile around 64.6 cm. No standard school seat fits that width, which is why DELTA has no side bars at all.

Lumbar support height: Dowell (1995, HFES annual meeting, 773 US adults) measured the lumbar curve's peak at about 24.8 cm (women) and 25.3 cm (men) above the seat, ranging from 15 to 36 cm.

## 3. Standards and sizing rules

**EN 1729-1** (European school furniture) sizes by height. Size 6 is for ages 14-18 (159-188 cm tall): seat 460 mm high and 400 mm deep, table 760 mm. Size 5 (146-177 cm): seat 430, table 710. It allows a seat-to-back angle of 95 to 110 degrees. Summary via KI Europe and SATRA: https://www.satra.com/spotlight/article.php?id=500

**Sizing rules from the school-furniture research:**

- Size the seat by popliteal height, not by age or stature, and exclude as few students as possible (Molenbroek 2003, *Ergonomics* 46(7)).
- Seat depth should be 80 to 95 % of buttock-to-knee length; beyond 95 % the front edge presses behind the knee (Parcells, Stommel and Hubbard 1999).
- In that same study of US middle-schoolers, the seat height didn't fit 61 % of students, the seat width 55 % and the desk height 52 %.
- Dynamic seating improved lower-back motor control in schoolchildren (Fettweis et al. 2018, *J. Back Musculoskelet. Rehabil.*).

**ANSI/BIFMA X6.1** (US educational seating): size C (seats 425 mm and up) is rated for a 125 kg (275 lb) user and tested with a 225 lbf back functional load and a 150 lbf proof load. Rear stability uses weights on the seat plus a push at the back; the related X5.1 formula gives F = 1.1 x (47 - seat height in inches) lbf. Summary: https://www.micomlab.com/micom-testing/bifma-x61/

**EN 1022** (seating stability) checks sideways overbalancing of seats without arms with a 600 N downward load near the side edge of the seat plus a 20 N sideways push. DELTA's tipping table uses those loads, and a harsher case of a heavy student leaning out over the side. Summary: https://catas.com/uploads/media/articolo-1022-eng.pdf

**Fingers and hair:** European furniture practice treats openings between about 8 and 25 mm as finger traps: make gaps smaller than 8 or larger than 25 (EN 12520, EN 16139). I found no published incident data on hair caught in school chairs, so DELTA uses the same rule for hair and avoids moving gaps near the head.

## 4. Similar products

- **Virco Analogy / Metaphor sled-base combo:** one-piece polypropylene shell that flexes, sled base, 19 x 25 in top. The closest Virco design to DELTA. https://virco.com/product/analogy-sled-based-combo-unit/
- **Virco Zuma combo:** 19-3/8 x 25-3/8 in top, 18 in seat, side support arm.
- **KI Intellect Wave:** one-piece shell whose back flexes passively; 26 x 19 in top; a "double entry" version you can get into from either side.
- **KI Postura+:** one-piece PP shell with gas-injected legs and a waterfall edge.
- **Smith System Flavors:** rivet-free PP shell whose 16 in back flexes so you can turn more easily; 275 lb rating.
- **Steelcase Node:** chair-desk with a worksurface that swings to either side (non-handed).

About 10 % of students are left-handed, and standard right-hand tablet desks force them into a hooked wrist; most makers answer with a separate left-hand model. DELTA's centred desk is the same for both.

## 5. Materials and 3D printing

- **Polypropylene impact copolymer** is the standard for flexing one-piece chair shells: flexural modulus about 1.0-1.5 GPa, tough, recyclable (code 5), typical walls 3-5 mm.
- **PA6-GF30 glass-filled nylon**, gas-assist moulded, is the standard material for office-chair bases (strong, hollow, light).
- **Moulded hard-plastic desktops** are seamless and colour-through. Laminate tops with glued edge banding are cheaper, but students pick the banding off.
- **PLA for flexing parts:** PLA is stiff and fairly brittle (elongation at break 4-10 %). Keep repeated flexing under about 1.5-2 % strain, make the flexing part long and thin, and print it so the bend runs along the printed lines, never across layers. TPU would be the material for a flexure that has to last forever.
- **FDM fits (0.4 mm nozzle):** about 0.15-0.3 mm per side for sliding, 0.05-0.2 for press fits; overhangs up to 45 degrees; bridges under 10 mm print cleanly; texture ribs at least 0.6-1 mm wide so the nozzle can draw them.
- **Two colours on a one-colour printer:** print each colour as separate parts, which is how the kit is split.
